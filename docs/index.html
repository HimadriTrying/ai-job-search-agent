3 rules she won't break

What makes her trustworthy?

Never fabricates

Every generated line is checked in code against a frozen record of your real history. Invent an employer or a metric and the build fails. A promise you can verify.

You commit, not the agent

She automates everything up to applying, reaching out, or accepting, then stops and hands you the wheel. No message leaves without your say-so.

Your data stays yours

Your real profile never leaves your machine. The public repo ships only templates, so anyone can fork it and fill in their own.

Good to know

FAQs

Where are my CV and profile stored?＋
On your machine. Lucy runs locally in Claude Code, and your real profile, work history, and application tracker are gitignored, so they never reach the public repo. The try page runs entirely in your browser and stores nothing.
How is my data handled?＋
There is no server of ours and no analytics on your documents. The only network call your data makes is to Anthropic's API using your own key or subscription, under Anthropic's terms. Your connections export, tracker, and profile are plain local files you own and can delete at any time.
What does Lucy cost?＋
Lucy is free and open source under the MIT license. You bring your own Claude access: a Claude subscription for interactive use, or an Anthropic API key if you want the scheduled morning sweeps. The only cost is your model usage.
Will she apply to jobs without me?＋
Never. Lucy stops at every point of commitment. Applications, outreach messages, and offer decisions always wait for your explicit go-ahead, behind 2 independent confirmation locks.
Can she invent things about me?＋
No. Every outbound document is checked in code against a frozen record of your real history before it can leave the system. An invented employer, credential, or metric fails loudly instead of shipping quietly.
Can I read or change how she works?＋
Yes. The whole system is open source on GitHub under the MIT license. Fork it, read every prompt and check, and adapt it to your own search.
